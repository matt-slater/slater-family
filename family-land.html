Family Land

Where the Slaters owned land, from the first Simsbury grants of the 1670s to Harry Slater's farm at Luthers Mills in 1950. Each circle marks a place named in a deed, grant, will or map. Its size shows how precisely the record places it. A dashed outline is a lot reconstructed from survey maps.

These are locations, not surveyed boundaries. Colonial deeds describe land by its neighbours, brooks and hills rather than measured lines, so most parcels can only be placed within half a mile to a mile or so. Exact outlines will need the deeds themselves, and the Burlington farms are still being researched.

New York: Amenia, Dutchess County, c.1780–1800

DateLand
by 1782–before 1808Part of lot 35, Great Nine Partners Patent (then Amenia, now southern North East), Dutchess Co., NY
John Slater (Jr.) · precision lot outline reconstructed, ±1 km; John's part within the lot not known
"John Slater of Amenia Precinct" sells Simsbury land in 1782. A deed of 1808–10 describes land "in the Town of Amenia … part of lot Number 35, in the great nine partners", beginning "at the south west corner of Noah Wheeler land that he bought of John Slater". Lot 35 was the second lot from the north in the eastern tier of the patent (lots 28–36, numbered south to north, each about 3,400 acres; Reed 1875). Richard Edsall's survey map of c.1740 shows it as 3,475 acres, between lot 36 (Hendrick Ten Eyck) and lot 34 (David Jamison). The outline drawn here divides the tier into nine equal strips between Amenia's south line and the old Amenia–North East line through Millerton (the land north of today's town line, called North Amenia, was moved to North East in 1823). The strips are bounded by the Stanford line on the west and the Oblong on the east. It agrees with Reed's placing of lot 32 at Amenia village and the Red Meeting House. The Wheeler family's Nine Partners land lay "just to the west of the Oblong Lot 65", near Coleman Station, which suggests the Slater–Wheeler land was at the east end of lot 35. That is a lead, not proof. His sister Sarah and her husband Noah Brown lived "in ye Great nine Partners in Amen[ia] Precincts" in 1783.
Source: Dutchess Co. deeds 1808–1810 (FamilySearch full text); Simsbury land records, 1782 and 1783 deeds (FamilySearch full text); Edsall, manuscript map of the Great Nine Partners Patent, c.1740 (NYPL); Newton Reed, Early History of Amenia (1875), pp. 6, 21–27; NRHP nomination, Thomas N. Wheeler Farm (1999); U.S. Census TIGER town boundaries (2024)

Pennsylvania, 1858–1950

DateLand
1858–1867Saw mill and 10.7 acres on Tuscarora Creek, Auburn Twp.
William J. Slater · precision ±1 mile
1858 county map shows "W. J. Slater" with a saw mill on Tuscarora Creek at the Bradford line. 1864 deed: 10.7 acres, part of George Porter's warrant, running "to the center of the road up Tuscarora creek"; sold 1867.
Source: Susquehanna Co. deeds vol. 42 pp. 291–292, DGS 008094331, image 1598; Map of Susquehanna County, 1858 (Library of Congress)
1869"H. Rogers & B. Slater, S. Mill", Mehoopany Creek
possibly William J. or Adolphus Slater · precision rough (measured across a map fold)
A saw mill shared with a Rogers. William J. later sold land to Joseph H. Rogers (1879). Not proven to be him.
Source: Map of Wyoming County, 1869 (Library of Congress)
1950Harry Slater's farm, north of the Luthers Mills bridge
Harry G. Slater · precision road certain; house within ~¼ mile
1950 census: "¼ mi. N of bridge on T552 at Luthers' mills" — the road north from the Sugar Creek bridge (today T-557 / Covered Bridge Rd). The 1957 topographic map shows dwellings on this road about 0.1 and 0.47 mile north of the bridge. Fits the family map's Slater parcel north of Route 6.
Source: 1950 U.S. census, Burlington Twp., ED 8-14, sheet 6; USGS Ulster 1:24,000 topographic map, 1957
1858"A. Slatter" house, north of Sugar Creek at East Burlington (Luthers Mills)
"A. Slatter" — possibly Adolphus D. Slater · precision ±0.2 mile; name reading uncertain
On the 1858 county map a house marked "A. Slatter"[?] stands on the road leading north-east out of East Burlington (the village now called Luthers Mills).
Source: Map of Bradford County, 1858 (Wm. J. Barker), PA State Archives Map #334, tile 2
1858"W. Sluter" house, south-east Burlington Twp.
"W. Sluter" — possibly William J. Slater · precision ±0.5 mile; name reading uncertain
On the 1858 county map, "W. Sluter" (perhaps W. Slater) is near E. Bennett, M. Bailey and the Stropes — names that also neighbour the Slaters in later censuses. About a mile south-west of Slater Hollow.
Source: Map of Bradford County, 1858 (Wm. J. Barker), PA State Archives Map #334, tile 6
—Slater Hollow
place name · precision named valley (USGS)
A valley named Slater Hollow, about 2 miles east-south-east of Luthers Mills, south of Sugar Creek. Adolphus Slater was "of Slater Hollow, Burlington Twp." (d. 1881).
Source: USGS Geographic Names (GNIS), via PASDA

Known from records but not yet placed

  • John Slater (1685): 20 acres "over the mountain", east of the Farmington River
  • John Slater (1687): Marsh on the south branch of Hop Brook "where Joshua Holcomb made Tarre" (still "Slater Marsh" in 1797)
  • John Slater (1713 inventory): 14 acres "in Slaters swamp"
  • Deacon John Slater (1709–1717): House lot east of the river by the Weatogue–Windsor road, with 60 acres "to the foot of the mountains"
  • John Slater Jr. (1768–1770): Hopmeadow home lot (4 acres) and 13⅓-acre meadow lot on the river, sold before he moved to Amenia, NY
  • William J. Slater (1867–1879): Land in Forkston Twp., Wyoming Co. (deed books not filmed; at the courthouse in Tunkhannock)
  • Edwin and Harry Slater (c.1880–1955): Exact farm boundaries and acreage in Burlington Twp. (Bradford deeds before 1970 are not online; courthouse in Towanda)
  • Hemenway family (c.1900): No Hemenway farm found in Burlington on the 1858 map; location not yet confirmed
  • Scouten family (1858): "C. Scouten" on the 1858 map in the far south-west of Burlington Twp., about 3 miles from Luthers Mills — not where the family map puts the Scouten land
