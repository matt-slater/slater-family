John Slater's 1664 Verses

In February and March 1664, verses mocking a Concord courtship and the town constable were posted on the Concord meetinghouse. On 8 March John Slater confessed to writing them. Both papers survive in the Middlesex County court files.

Content warning: both poems are bawdy, and the second is very crude. They are transcribed in full because they are the only surviving writing in John Slater's own hand from his Concord years.

Spelling and line breaks follow the manuscript. [Brackets] mark uncertain readings; [?] marks words I could not read. The scans are about 200 dpi and partly stained, so many readings are uncertain. View the originals on FamilySearch (free sign-in): verse 1, verse 2, confession.

What the poems say

Verse 1 opens like a town crier's notice, announcing the planned match of Mary Edwards and "Sir Thomas Pinion", written 14 February and posted 1 March. After a mock-grand appeal to the Muse Melpomene ("Milpomyne"), it turns into bawdy comedy about the couple, ending with a run of mock "remedies" Pinion supposedly offers.

Verse 2 attacks constable John Heywood, who had been searching young women after a maid was found with child. It mocks him for doing it for money rather than conscience. A shorter verse on the same sheet defends the writer ("I throw out my orthographie ... so I end"), and a damaged prose note calls Heywood "the cheate actor in this case".

The confession, 8 March 1663/4

Report of the Concord commissioners, film 007902664, image 426. This is the evidence that John Slater wrote the verses.

8. march: 63 64: A cause brought before us the Comissioners of Concorde of lybills writ in papers & set upon a post on ye meetinghouse; (we) were informed that John Slater was suspected to be ye writer of them whereupon we sent for him before us; John heywood being on[e] mentioned in ye lybills who brought him before us; did say to Slater that if he would freely confesse that he wrote them he will freely forgive him; upon examination of him he was in a humbill frame & we discerned [him] that som others did joyne with him; we said to him if he will discover all; we will promise him to show him what faviour we could; whereupon he did confesse that he did writ them; & was sory that he had done it, & that it was ye first time that he had done so, & should be ye last; & testified his sorrow & shame of the thing in a writing which he set upon ye meetinghouse, to ye view of all; this was heard, & promised, before us; & it being ye first time of him, & who[?] it will be ye last; have passed it by for this time; if it may please ye honoured Court to doe the same; Robert meriam Tho: T Brooke his marke Georg Wheeler (Docketed below: "1663/4 March 8th Case of John Slater … Concord")
